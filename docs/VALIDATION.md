# Validation against external references

Until this suite existed, every physics test compared the code with theory (thin-airfoil
theory, elliptic-wing results) or with itself. `src/physics/benchmarks/` compares the model
with things it was **not** built from: wind-tunnel measurements, two independent codes and
published aircraft figures. Run it with:

```
npm test                    # the checks run as ordinary vitest tests
npm run benchmark           # prints the table below (add -- --markdown for Markdown)
```

**Summary: 171 checks. 131 pass, 40 are documented known deviations, 0 fail.** The inviscid
wing solver agrees with AVL almost exactly. The 2D section model gets lift, zero-lift angle,
maximum lift and pitching moment right within teaching tolerances. Its main weaknesses are a
lift slope about 5 % too steep, conservative minimum drag, and the high-speed buffet and
drag-divergence estimates for the 737.

## Method

- **Tolerances first.** `tolerances.ts` fixes how close a teaching-grade model should come for
  each quantity, with the reason in a comment. It was committed before any model value was
  compared (commit "fix tolerances and add numerical reference fixtures"). The two 3D tolerances
  added later (stall angle ±3 deg, yes/no behaviour) were also set before the 3D runs.
- **No silent loosening.** A check that misses its tolerance is listed in `knownDeviations.ts`
  with the reason. It runs as `it.fails`, so the suite stays green and the gap stays visible.
  If a later fix makes it pass, the test turns red until the entry is removed.
- **Same reduction on both sides.** 2D polars of the model and of NeuralFoil are reduced
  identically:
  - lift slope and zero-lift angle: a straight-line fit over [α_L0 − 2, α_L0 + 6] deg;
  - cl_max and stall angle: the first lift peak;
  - cd_min: the minimum over [α_L0 − 4, α_L0 + 10] deg;
  - cm_c/4: the mean over the linear range.
- **Same geometry on both sides.** The 3D fixtures store the sections that were given to AVL.
  A test checks that `buildWingGeometry` still produces exactly those sections, and that the
  737 benchmark wing is still the `b737-800` preset wing (winglet removed).
- **Reynolds and Mach numbers matched.** Tunnel cases are rebuilt at the test's Reynolds number
  (on the MAC) and Mach number. The planform is scaled at sea level so the MAC gives the test
  Reynolds number at the test speed; coefficients do not depend on scale.
- **Fixtures.** Reference data are committed JSON in `src/physics/benchmarks/reference/`, each
  file with a `source` field. Numerical references are regenerated by
  `scripts/benchmarks/generate_reference.py`, run in an isolated Python environment with
  `neuralfoil`, `aerosandbox` and `optvl`. The TypeScript tests never need Python.

## Sources

### 2D experiments (`naca_2d_experimental.json`)

1. I. H. Abbott, A. E. von Doenhoff, L. S. Stivers, _Summary of Airfoil Data_, NACA Report 824, 1945. Appendix V figure pages:
   - NACA 0006: p. 131
   - NACA 2412: p. 136 (test Re 3.1, 5.7, 8.9 million)
   - NACA 4412: p. 141
   - NACA 64-006: p. 177

   These are the same charts as Abbott & von Doenhoff, _Theory of Wing Sections_ (Dover 1959),
   Appendix IV. https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19930090976/downloads/19930090976.pdf

   Report 824 prints its section data only as charts. They were digitised for this project from
   the NTRS scan, with the axes calibrated on the printed grid. Accuracy is about:
   - cl_max ±0.02
   - stall angle ±0.5 deg
   - cd ±0.0002
   - a0 ±0.003/deg
   - α_L0 ±0.2 deg
   - cm ±0.005

2. C. L. Ladson, _Effects of Independent Variation of Mach and Reynolds Numbers on the
   Low-Speed Aerodynamic Characteristics of the NACA 0012 Airfoil Section_, NASA TM-4074, 1988.
   Values are read from the printed Table I (M = 0.15, free transition).
   https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19880019495/downloads/19880019495.pdf
3. L. K. Loftin, K. S. Cohen, NACA TN 1591, 1948, Fig. 15. This is the NACA 0012 data that
   Abbott & von Doenhoff use; Report 824 has no 0012 chart. Values are read from the figure.
   https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19930082240/downloads/19930082240.pdf

### 2D numerical (`neuralfoil_2d.json`)

NeuralFoil 0.3.3 (P. Sharpe, https://github.com/peterdsharpe/NeuralFoil) is a neural-network
surrogate trained on XFOIL.

- Model "xxxlarge", n_crit 9, Mach 0.
- The airfoil contour comes from the app's own NACA equations.
- NACA 0006, 0012, 2412 and 4412 at Re 3, 6 and 9 million.
- Plain flaps (25 % chord, 10 and 20 deg, NACA 0012 and 2412, Re 6 million) are supported by
  deflecting the geometry with AeroSandbox `Airfoil.add_control_surface`. NeuralFoil's own
  confidence on those geometries is 0.6–0.9.

### 3D numerical (`vlm_3d.json`)

- **AVL** (M. Drela, H. Youngren, MIT), run through **OptVL 2.5.0** (MDO Lab,
  https://github.com/mdolab/OptVL), which bundles AVL as a Python extension.
  - 16 chordwise × 96 spanwise vortices per semispan, both cosine-spaced.
  - CDi and e come from AVL's Trefftz-plane values (CDff, e).
  - Mesh convergence against 8 × 48: CL_alpha changes by under 0.1 %.
- **AeroSandbox 4.2.10** `VortexLatticeMethod` (https://github.com/peterdsharpe/AeroSandbox) is a
  second lattice code. It is reported here but not asserted.
- **Geometry subtlety found while building the suite.** Between sections, AVL interpolates
  chord × incidence (a ruled loft). The app interpolates twist linearly in span. On a tapered
  wing with washout this changes the twist distribution itself: with 3 deg washout and taper
  0.4, CL at α = 0 differed by 35 %. The generator therefore splits every segment into 8
  linearly interpolated sections, so both codes see the same wing.
- **Benchmark wings:**

| Id                     | Planform                                                                                                                                 |
| ---------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `rect-ar6`             | rectangular, AR 6, NACA 0012                                                                                                             |
| `taper-ar8`            | taper 0.4, unswept quarter chord, AR 8                                                                                                   |
| `swept35-ar6`          | 35 deg quarter-chord sweep, taper 0.4, AR 6                                                                                              |
| `b737-800-wing`        | the `b737-800` preset base wing: AR 10.3, 25 deg sweep, inboard trailing-edge extension, 6 deg dihedral, 3 deg washout, cambered section |
| `b737-800-wing-m0.785` | the same wing at Mach 0.785 (Prandtl-Glauert in both codes)                                                                              |

AVL vs AeroSandbox CL_alpha:

| Wing          | AVL (1/rad) | AeroSandbox (1/rad) | Difference |
| ------------- | ----------- | ------------------- | ---------- |
| rect-ar6      | 4.206       | 4.270               | +1.5 %     |
| taper-ar8     | 4.736       | 4.785               | +1.0 %     |
| swept35-ar6   | 3.922       | 3.967               | +1.2 %     |
| b737-800-wing | 5.075       | 5.357               | +5.6 %     |

AeroSandbox runs 1–1.5 % high on the simple wings and 5.6 % high on the 737 wing. On the 737
wing's own two-section loft it failed outright (CL_alpha ≈ 46/rad). AVL is therefore the
asserted reference.

### 3D experiments (`swept_wing_experimental.json`)

1. W. Koven, R. R. Graham, _Wind-Tunnel Investigation of High-Lift and Stall-Control Devices on
   a 37° Sweptback Wing of Aspect Ratio 6 at High Reynolds Numbers_, NACA RM L8D29, 1948.
   https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19930093752
   - **Geometry:** AR 6, taper 0.5, no twist, 35.2 deg quarter-chord sweep (calculated from the
     37.25 deg leading edge). The section is NACA 64₁-212 normal to the 0.27c line.
   - **Model stand-in section:** 4-digit, 1 % camber at 50 %, 9.8 % thick streamwise.
   - **Test conditions:** R = 6.8 million, M ≈ 0.13.
   - **Values used:** CL_alpha 0.070/deg (text); CLmax 1.27 at 19.0 deg (Table I); the outer
     half of the semispan stalls first (text).
2. J. F. Cahill, S. M. Gottlieb, _Low-Speed Aerodynamic Characteristics of a Series of Swept
   Wings Having NACA 65A006 Airfoil Sections_, NACA RM L50F16, 1950.
   https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19930086214/downloads/19930086214.pdf
   - **Wing:** 45-6-0.6, i.e. 45 deg quarter-chord sweep, AR 6, taper 0.6. The stand-in section
     is NACA 0006.
   - **Values used, read from figures:** CL_alpha ≈ 0.054/deg (Fig. 18, R = 6 million); CLmax
     ≈ 1.02 (Fig. 19a, R = 3 million); tip separation causes the pitch break (text).

### Aircraft (`aircraft_published.json`)

- **Reference areas:**
  - 737-800: 124.6 m², from https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_737_Next_Generation
  - 747-400: 525 m², from https://aerospaceweb.org/aircraft/jetliner/b747 and
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_747-400
- **Cruise CL:** calculated from published-class inputs (L = W, ISA):
  - 737: 65 t, FL350, M 0.785 gives 0.497 (range 0.48–0.51)
  - 747: 330 t, FL350, M 0.855 gives 0.505
- **Landing CLmax:** R. K. Rudolph, _High-Lift Systems on Commercial Subsonic Airliners_, NASA
  CR-4746, 1996, https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19960052267. Fig. 2.29 gives the approach CL
  (737-X 1.75; 747-400 1.36), and p. 40 gives CLmax ≈ 1.54 × CL_approach. That makes 2.7 and
  2.1.
- **Buffet onset:** an **estimate**. It is 1.3 × CL at the 1.3 g maximum cruise altitude, and
  those altitudes were not verified. No public Boeing buffet boundary was found; Obert's
  _Aerodynamic Design of Transport Aircraft_ has the charts but is not online.
- **Drag-divergence Mach:** a **proxy**. No M_dd is published, so the typical cruise Mach is
  used, because airliners cruise near M_dd. MMO is 0.82 for the 737-800 and 0.92 for the
  747-400.

## What is well validated

- **The vortex-lattice solver.** Against AVL on five wings, including the 737 wing with an
  inboard trailing-edge extension, dihedral, washout and camber, and at Mach 0.785:
  - CL_alpha agrees to 0.1 %;
  - CL at 4 deg to 0.003;
  - CDi to 1 %;
  - span efficiency e to 0.004.

  The lift, induced drag and lift distribution drawn in the tunnel are as good as a
  research-grade lattice code's.

- **Zero-lift angle and pitching moment.** Zero-lift angle is within 0.4 deg of the tunnel and
  0.1 deg of NeuralFoil. cm_c/4 is within 0.017 of the tunnel and 0.009 of NeuralFoil. The model
  cm is the inviscid one, so it is 0.015 more nose-down than measured on the cambered sections,
  as expected.
- **cl_max and stall angle of NACA 4-digit sections.** cl_max is within 10 % of the measured
  smooth-model values from Re 3 to 9 million (0012 and 2412 within 7 %; 4412 runs 8–10 % high).
  That includes the thin 0006, whose leading-edge stall the empirical table reproduces. Measured
  stall angles are matched within 1.6 deg.
- **Plain-flap lift increments and flapped cl_max.** Within 15 % of NeuralFoil.
- **Whole-wing lift slope of a 35 deg swept transport-like wing** (RM L8D29). Within 3.3 %.
  CLmax is within 16 %, and the stall starts on the outboard half, as measured.
- **Aircraft level.**
  - Cruise CL: 737-800 within 10 %, 747-400 within 4 %.
  - Landing CLmax with flaps and slats: within 8 % and 1.4 %.
  - 747-400 buffet onset: within 3 %.
  - 747-400 drag-divergence Mach: within 0.003.

## Structural limits (not tested, because nothing comparable exists)

- **Wing only.** No fuselage, tail, nacelles or pylons. Published airplane CLmax and buffet
  boundaries include the tail download and fuselage, so they are compared only after rescaling
  to the published area, with wide tolerances.
- **NACA 4-digit stand-ins.** The 737's and 747's real sections are supercritical or
  transonic-transport sections, and the 64- and 65-series test wings are shown with 4-digit
  sections of the same thickness and similar camber. Section effects beyond thickness and
  camber, such as aft loading and the 6-series drag bucket, are absent.
- **No wake roll-up.** The wake is flat and runs along +x, as in AVL. This is fine for lift and
  induced drag at moderate CL; it does not capture the high-alpha vortex behaviour of very
  swept wings.
- **Empirical stall and post-stall.** cl_max comes from a thickness table, Reynolds power law,
  camber bonus and flap/slat rules, fitted to Abbott & von Doenhoff. Results outside NACA
  4-digit-like sections are extrapolation.
- **Compressibility.** Prandtl-Glauert in the lattice (it agrees with AVL at Mach 0.785), plus
  the Korn equation and Lock wave drag for M_dd, M_crit and buffet. These are handbook
  estimates, good to about ±0.02–0.05 in Mach.

## What the numbers mean for a user

- **Trustworthy:** lift curves, induced drag, span efficiency and the lift distribution along
  the span, including how sweep, taper, twist, dihedral and Mach number change them. These are
  as good as a research vortex-lattice code. Zero-lift angles and moments are also trustworthy,
  as is the size of flap lift increments.
- **Read as "about right":** maximum lift (within about 10–15 %), stall angle (within about
  2–4 deg), and drag at cruise. Profile drag errs on the high side for very smooth sections. The
  2D lift slope is about 5 % steep.
- **Qualitative only:** where and how a swept wing stalls (the tip goes first, as in the
  tunnel, but a little early and abruptly), high-speed buffet, drag-divergence Mach for the 737
  family, and anything on thin, highly swept wings at high angle of attack (vortex lift is
  missing).
